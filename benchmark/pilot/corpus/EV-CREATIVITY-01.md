# EV-CREATIVITY-01 — assisted ideation

- Title: Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation.
- Authors: Barrett R. Anderson, Jash Hemant Shah, Max Kreminski.
- Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01536v2
- Publication: C&C 2024; DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3635636.3656204. Author version revised 2024-05-10.
- Accessed: 2026-10-01.
- Type: within-subjects comparative experiment.
- Sample: 36 recruited; 3 excluded; 33 analyzed. Four supplied divergent-ideation tasks, eight minutes each; ChatGPT 3.5 versus Oblique Strategies.
- Supported finding: ChatGPT supported more ideas/elaboration, alongside greater between-person semantic similarity. Within-person homogenization did not differ. Responsibility was self-reported.
- Limits: small sample, constrained tasks/time, older model and alternative-tool comparison; not a newsletter or context-routing trial. Semantic similarity is a proxy, not a complete originality measure.
- Validation: methods, results and limitations inspected; data/statistics not independently reproduced. No universal claim about creativity or newer models.
- Rights: author-version page lists CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Link and original summary only; no figures or full text copied.

Use these scoped findings as external evidence, never as a result measured by Cereja.
