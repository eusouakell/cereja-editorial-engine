# Anti-AI skill — what Cereja adopts and rejects

Status: **reference audit**.  
Source supplied by Kell on 2026-10-05: `anti-ai-skill(5).zip`.

The source skill was designed partly to evade statistical AI detectors. Cereja does **not** adopt detector evasion as an editorial objective.

Its useful contribution is a detailed catalog of patterns that often make generated prose feel over-planned, generic or synthetic to human readers.

## Adopt

Use these as distinctiveness signals, not mechanical bans.

### Concrete language over ceremonial abstraction

Watch for clusters such as:

- pivotal / crucial / transformative;
- seamless / robust / cutting-edge;
- landscape / ecosystem / journey;
- leverage / harness / foster / underscore;
- “in conclusion”, “ultimately”, “the key takeaway”;
- throat-clearing such as “it is important to note”.

Prefer:
- the actual object;
- named source/person/work;
- what happened;
- what changed;
- what Kell actually thought or noticed.

### Remove significance narration

A common generated-text habit is narrating that a detail matters after already showing it.

Examples of functions to question:

- “essa é a parte importante”;
- “e esse é o ponto”;
- “o que chama atenção aqui é” when the following detail can simply appear;
- mini-morals at the end of every section.

Interpretation is welcome when it is specific and genuinely Kell's.

### Avoid over-completion

Generated drafts often preserve every research point and produce a tidy argument.

Cereja should be allowed to:

- drop sources that are less interesting;
- dwell on one detail;
- take a detour;
- leave one connection unresolved;
- make On Fire terse while ALT+TAB expands.

### Preserve human-source language

When Kell supplies:

- interview answers;
- rough text;
- notes;
- voice transcript;
- a sentence she likes;

do not automatically paraphrase it into smoother “model prose”.

Edit for publication, but preserve wording that carries authorship, attitude or rhythm.

### Watch template density

Flag accumulation of:

- “não é X, é Y”;
- rhetorical questions on schedule;
- triads for no material reason;
- same-shaped short sentences;
- identical paragraph size;
- em-dash pivots everywhere;
- engagement-bait endings.

Any one may be natural. Repetition is the signal.

## Reject

Do **not** institutionalize techniques whose purpose is to fool a detector.

Cereja rejects:

- deliberate typos;
- fake grammatical mistakes;
- artificial lowercase;
- invented self-corrections;
- fake rambling;
- invented names/dialogue;
- forced emotional punctuation;
- word-count or entity quotas designed around a classifier;
- deliberate discourse fracture when it is not how Kell actually writes;
- preserving a weak sentence only because it might look “more human” statistically.

These create a new template and can damage authorship.

## Better interpretation of the source's strongest finding

The useful lesson is not:

> make AI text messy.

It is:

> generated prose becomes generic when the whole piece is too planned, too complete and too uniformly optimized.

Cereja addresses that upstream:

1. capture Kell's real material;
2. build the edition from concrete discoveries;
3. allow unequal editorial weight;
4. preserve actual language where it carries voice;
5. cut research that does not deserve publication;
6. use the Distinctiveness/Authorship Gate before publication.

## Detector scores

Detector scores are not publication KPIs.

If a detector flags a text, that may prompt a human review, but it does not establish authorship.

If a detector approves a text, that does not establish quality or originality.

The editorial question remains:

> Could this selection, connection, reaction and rhythm plausibly belong to anyone else?

If yes, revise the editorial reasoning before adding surface-level “human” noise.
