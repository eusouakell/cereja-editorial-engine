# Narrative Craft & Oral Storytelling

**Status:** pilot guide  
**Purpose:** improve attention, comprehension, memory and authorship without turning every piece into a formulaic story.

## Core rule

> Story is a communication choice, not a mandatory container.

Before applying story craft, ask:

1. Is there a meaningful change, tension, discovery or sequence here?
2. Does narrative improve understanding or merely delay the point?
3. Would an argument, reference, diagram, list or demonstration be stronger?

If story does not add value, do not force it.

## Working craft library

### Tension, stakes and change

Study:
- The Moth — scene, desire/dilemma, stakes, conflict, change;
- This American Life / Brian Reed — action + reflection + stakes;
- Robert McKee — gap between expectation and result;
- Nancy Duarte — contrast between what is and what could be.

Use for:
- openings;
- case studies;
- executive interpretation;
- postmortems;
- presentations.

### Story first, technology second

Pixar's documented principle is useful for AI-assisted production: technology should enrich the story rather than drive story decisions.

Use as a guard against:
- format-first content;
- tool demos with no editorial point;
- novelty becoming the thesis.

### Brazilian narrative — situated voice, oral thought and observation

#### Conceição Evaristo
Study how writing can begin from situated experience, memory and where the writer's feet are planted.

Guard: **escrevivência is not a generic content framework.** Do not detach the concept from its historical, cultural and authorial context.

#### Ailton Krenak
Study thought developed through speech, association, image and oral rhythm before becoming edited text.

Useful for:
- YouTube;
- speaking;
- interview;
- essay development.

#### José Hamilton Ribeiro
Study field presence, lived reporting, observation and the discipline of being where the story happens.

Useful for:
- interviews;
- case studies;
- reporting;
- research-led content.

#### Rolando Boldrin
Study the Brazilian `causo` and oral performance through:
- voice;
- body;
- space;
- presence;
- timing;
- memory;
- character detail.

Useful for video and live storytelling, without imitating accent or persona.

#### Inezita Barroso
Study cultural mediation: how deep repertoire can invite an audience into a world without flattening it into a lesson.

#### Emicida
Study montage and cultural sampling: personal experience, archive, music, history and apparently distant references combined into a new reading.

Useful for:
- Cereja rabbit holes;
- visual essays;
- YouTube;
- cultural/technology connections.

### Performance + comedy study queue

Use these references primarily to study mechanics, not to copy worldview or legacy material:

- Jair Rodrigues — presence, rhythm, audience relationship;
- Moacyr Franco — register switching, variety, character and showmanship;
- Ronald Golias — character, comic timing and physical performance;
- Ary Toledo — setup/payoff and compact anecdotal structure;
- José Vasconcellos — spoken comedy, character and performance;
- Ariano Suassuna — oral rhythm, humor, performance and popular narrative.

Legacy humor may include stereotypes or conventions that should not be reproduced. Extract craft only after a current editorial/inclusion review.

## Story potential fields

For a candidate story route, record:

- **question/tension:** what remains unresolved?
- **stakes:** why should the audience care?
- **actor/desire:** who wants or needs something?
- **expectation:** what did we think would happen?
- **turn:** what changed the reading?
- **concrete material:** scene, object, person, quote, observation, artifact or data point;
- **action:** what happened?
- **reflection:** what does it mean?
- **ending state:** what does the audience now understand differently?
- **place/position:** from where is this being told?
- **oral test:** would a person naturally tell this, or does it sound manufactured?

## Narrative gate

When a story route is selected, review:

1. **Tension** — there is a real question, not clickbait.
2. **Stakes** — significance is legible.
3. **Concreteness** — the piece contains something observable.
4. **Progression** — understanding changes as the piece moves.
5. **Turn** — a contrast, discovery or change earns attention.
6. **Meaning** — story leads to interpretation, not anecdote for its own sake.
7. **Place** — perspective/context is not falsely universalized.
8. **Listening** — evidence is allowed to challenge the initial thesis.
9. **Voice** — language sounds tellable and authored.
10. **Anti-formula** — the framework is not visible as a template.

A piece may pass the editorial gate with **no story route** when another form is stronger.

## Channel translation

### Newsletter
Allow action ↔ reflection, rabbit holes and delayed synthesis when the path earns it.

### Instagram
Treat storytelling as visual sequencing: object → detail → context/turn → interpretation → exit.

### LinkedIn
Prefer gap/contrast and professional consequence. Avoid manufactured personal anecdotes.

### Medium
Use essayistic narrative when a durable argument benefits from scene, experience, evidence and reflection.

### DEV.to
Use build narrative: goal → assumption → implementation → unexpected result → investigation → change → learning.

### YouTube
Story craft is central. Prefer spoken structure, demonstration, presence, pauses and visual evidence over reading an essay to camera.
